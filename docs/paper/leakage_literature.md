# Look-ahead leakage in LLM forecasting: literature notes for the paper

Collected 2026-10-12 to justify the Study 4 leak layers. Each entry: what the paper does and what it implies
for this study. Check the current version of each working paper before citing specific numbers.

## The problem

- **Lopez-Lira, Tang and Zhu (2025), "The Memorization Problem: Can We Trust LLMs' Economic Forecasts?"**
  arXiv 2504.14765; SSRN 5217505. Formalise memorisation as non-identification: once a model saw the outcome
  in training, its counterfactual forecasting skill cannot be recovered from its outputs. GPT-4o recalls
  exact values of economic series before its cutoff and none after. Masking fails (the model rebuilds firm,
  quarter and year from anonymised earnings calls). Prompting the model to ignore later information fails
  (similar accuracy before and after a fake cutoff). Recommend chronologically consistent models or
  post-cutoff evaluation, and treat any memorisation evidence as a lower bound.
  *Implication: no pre-cutoff Haiku result in this study is identified skill; the paper says so.*
- **Sarkar and Vafa (2024), "Lookahead Bias in Pretrained Language Models."** Evidence of look-ahead in
  earnings-call risk factors (pandemic mentioned before 2020) and close elections. Main remedy: models trained
  only on text written before the analysis period.
- **Crane, Karra and Soto (2025), "Total Recall? Evaluating the Macroeconomic Knowledge of Large Language
  Models,"** FEDS 2025-044, Federal Reserve Board. LLMs recall macro data precisely but blend vintages and
  reference periods, and often believe data were released before they were. Note that many LLM economics
  papers address look-ahead with anonymisation, post-cutoff comparisons and prompting.
- **Ludwig, Mullainathan and Rambachan (2025), "Large Language Models: An Applied Econometric Framework,"**
  NBER WP 33344; Annual Review of Economics (2026). Prediction uses of LLM outputs are valid only with no
  leakage between the model's training data and the research sample; enforce it through model choice and
  research design.

## Mitigation and diagnostics

- **Glasserman and Lin (2023), "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated by GPT
  Sentiment Analysis,"** arXiv 2309.17322. Remove company identifiers from headlines; in sample the
  anonymised version did better, which they attribute to a distraction effect more than to look-ahead.
  *Implication: our masking follows this; Lopez-Lira et al. show its limits.*
- **Engelberg, Manela, Mullins and Vulicevic (2025), "Entity Neutering,"** SSRN 5182756. LLM agents mask and
  paraphrase text until an independent LLM cannot identify the firm; the gap between raw and neutered
  sentiment bounds the look-ahead bias, larger for long texts and volatile periods.
  *Implication: our dating tests (Test A, memory exam) measure how far masking falls short of neutering.*
- **Gao, Jiang and Yan (2025), "Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts"** (first titled "A Test of
  Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts"), arXiv 2512.23847. Lookahead Propensity: how strongly the model recalls
  the outcome from a date-only query; a positive interaction between it and forecast accuracy signals
  contamination; the effect vanishes after the training cutoff.
  *Implication: our memory flag adapts this with the memory exam's recall result (no token probabilities
  from the API).*
- **He, Lv, Manela and Wu (2025), "Chronologically Consistent Large Language Models,"** arXiv 2502.21206.
  ChronoBERT and ChronoGPT trained only on text available at each date; in their return test the
  look-ahead effect is modest and model- and application-specific.
  *Implication: ChronoBERT is our leak-free reference reader.*
- **Li, Wang and Ma (2026), "Summoning the Oracle to Slay It" (FinCAD),** arXiv 2605.24564. Inference-time
  decoding that damps memorised outcomes; evaluated on 7-14B open models. Not usable through a closed API.
- **Benhenda (2026), "Look-Ahead-Bench,"** arXiv 2601.13770. Standard open LLMs show look-ahead bias in
  financial workflows; point-in-time models show less.
- **Kong et al. (2026), "Evaluating LLMs in Finance Requires Explicit Bias Consideration,"** arXiv 2602.14233.
  Look-ahead among five recurring biases in financial LLM work.

## LLMs and the Fed

- **Hansen and Kazinnik (2023/2024), "Can ChatGPT Decipher Fedspeak?"** Richmond Fed. GPT models classify the
  stance of FOMC statement sentences better than BERT and dictionaries.
- **Chan-Lau, Quach and Shi (2025), "Forecasting Federal Fund Rates with AI: LSTM, GRU, and Large Language
  Model Approaches,"** AMRO WP/25-10. LSTM/GRU tuned by Bayesian optimisation with five-fold time-series CV
  inside the pre-2016 training set; zero-shot Claude 3.5 Sonnet. They flag look-ahead as a limitation and
  note that masking dates gives limited protection. Our earlier GRU settings (188 units, 2e-4, 449 epochs,
  12 steps) were theirs; Study 4 tunes on our own data instead.
- FedSight AI (arXiv 2512.15728) and MiniFed (arXiv 2410.18012): multi-agent FOMC simulations; small test
  sets (FedSight: 16 meetings).
